Exemption Issues

Healthcare Exemption services

Healthcare exemption services are statutory provisions that grant specific vulnerable groups free-of-charge medical care at public health facilities to bypass user fees and cost-sharing models. These systems target demographics with high medical risks or severe financial constraints to ensure equitable access to essential treatments.

Patients / Clients Categories for Exemption on Health Services

  • Prisons
  • GBV and VAC (Gender Based Violence & Violence Against Children) Group
  • Disabled People
  • Chronic Diseases People
  • Children Under 5 years
  • Pregnant Women

Criteria for Exemption on Healthcare Services

  • Exemption service for Above 60 Years Elders should come with their Elders Registration Cards or Letter of Introduction from Local Government Authority in Corresponding to their Legal Passport Sizes
  • Exemption service for Under 5 Years Children, Their Parents should come with their Child Clinic Cards / their Children's Birth Certificates
  • Exemption service for Disabled People should come with their Letter of Introduction form Local Government Authority in corresponding with their legal Passport sizes
  • Exemption service for RCH Pregnant Women should come with their Child Clinic Cards

Chronic Disease People

Chronic diseases are long-lasting health conditions lasting one year or more that require ongoing medical care and limit daily activities. Main examples include heart disease, cancer, diabetes, and chronic lung diseases. They are the leading cause of death, disability, and high healthcare spending globally.

Common Types of Chronic Conditions

  • Cardiovascular Disease: Heart attacks, strokes, and high blood pressure.
  • Cancers: Malignant tumors including lung and colorectal cancers.
  • Diabetes: Type 1 and Type 2 metabolic sugar regulation failures.
  • Respiratory Illnesses: Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and asthma

Core Risk Factors: Tobacco Use- Smoking causes roughly 480,000 deaths yearly in the U.S. alone and damages vascular and lung tissue.Poor Diet & Inactivity: Contributes heavily to obesity, high blood pressure, and high cholesterol.Alcohol Abuse: Excess use drives liver and heart damage alongside cancer risks.Environmental Exposure: Air pollution accounts for millions of noncommunicable disease deaths globally.

Healthcare Management Strategies: Early Screening: Routine check-ups to catch markers like high glucose or elevated blood pressure early.Lifestyle Changes: Diet adjustment, smoking cessation programs, and increased physical movement.Care Coordination: Multidisciplinary teams tracking patient metrics over long periods rather than short acute visits.

RCH / Pregnant Women

Pregnancy care in the health system involves regular check-ups, screenings, and support from doctors or nurses to keep both mother and baby healthy. Globally, timely medical management helps prevent major risks like high blood pressure, bleeding, and infections.

Routine Check-ups and ScheduleEarly visits: Once a month during weeks 4 through 28.Mid-pregnancy: Twice a month during weeks 28 through 36.Late pregnancy: Weekly from week 36 until birth.Core checks: Blood pressure, weight, urine tests, and tracking fetal growth.

Healthcare exemption services for pregnant women are public policy measures designed to remove financial barriers, ensuring free access to essential reproductive, maternal, and newborn health services at public health facilities regardless of a patient's economic status
