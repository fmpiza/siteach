Birth Certificates Registration Issues

In Tanzania, birth certificates are managed and issued by the Registration Insolvency and Trusteeship Agency (RITA). By law, births should be registered within 90 days on the Tanzanian mainland. First-time registration for young children is often conducted via special local or health-campaign initiatives, while standard and late applications are processed physically or digitally

Birth Certificates Registration Requirements

  • Timeframe: Must be completed within 90 days of birth on Tanzania Mainland.
  • Hospital Birth Notification: Official paper or electronic document provided by the health facility or clinic.
  • Parent Identification: Copies of parent national IDs, passports, or official identification.
  • Marriage Proof: Parents' marriage certificate (if applicable).
  • Cost: Often free or heavily subsidized for children under 5 years old under specific government/UNICEF-backed initiatives.

Registration Deadlines and Costs

Standard Registration (Under 90 days): Fee is approximately Tsh 7,000 to Tsh 8,000 depending on the application route. First-time registration drives for under-fives at health facilities are frequently free.

Late Registration (1 to 10 years late): Fee is Tsh 8,000.

Late Registration (Over 10 years late): Fee is Tsh 20,000

Key Legal and Judicial Trends

Tanzanian courts handle gender-based violence (GBV) and sexual offenses under strict penal laws, yielding judgments that frequently range from heavy prison terms (30 years to life imprisonment) to acquittals when evidence like medical forms (PF3) or age proof fall short. Landmark rulings—such as the Court of Appeal's 2019 decision raising the legal marriage age to 18—fundamentally shape GBV jurisprudence

Conviction Sentences: Serious GBV and sexual violence cases adjudicated in the High Court and District Courts regularly result in custodial sentences between 30 years and life imprisonment.Evidentiary Standards: Recent appellate and High Court judgments (tracked via platforms like TanzLII) emphasize that strict proof of penetration, assailant identity, and medical verification (such as PF3 forms) are mandatory to sustain convictions.Victim Compensation: Courts occasionally award restitution or compensation to survivors (e.g., ranging from TZS 100,000 to over 1,000,000/=), though consistency in quantum remains variable.Systemic Challenges: Studies show that only a fraction of reported community GBV incidents successfully culminate in formal court convictions due to evidentiary gaps or out-of-court settlements

As for the prevalence rates, a number of international studies on GBV, in particular the 2001 WHO Study on GBV, suggested that GBV was widely prevalent in each of the Sub-Saharan African (SSA) countries. The major types of GBV included domestic violence/ intimate partner violence, female genital mutilation (FGM), sexual violence and violence against elderly women.

Legal Punishment Against Violence against Children

In Tanzania, the legal framework addressing Violence Against Children (VAC) spans criminal penalties for severe abuse alongside controversial statutory allowances for certain types of physical chastisement. While severe offenses like child rape or severe physical abuse carry heavy prison sentences under the Penal Code and the Law of the Child Act, traditional corporal punishment remains legally permitted under specific regulatory frameworks

Legal Protections and Severe PenaltiesSexual Violence and Rape: Prosecuted heavily under penal laws, with statutory sentences for child defilement and rape often ranging up to life imprisonment.Torture and Inhuman Treatment: Article 13 of the Law of the Child Act formally prohibits torture, cruel punishment, or degrading treatment.National Action Plans: Guided by frameworks like the National Plan of Action to End Violence Against Women and Children (NPA-VAWC), aiming to shift enforcement and community response

Severe Custodial Sentences: Trial courts hand down fixed or life imprisonment terms (such as life imprisonment for aggravated sexual offenses or severe statutory rape).Imprisonment for Assault: General physical abuse or severe misdemeanor violence results in jail terms scaling from several months up to multiple years under penal codes.Judicial Corporal Punishment: For specific violent crimes or offenses involving young offenders, courts can legally administer corporal punishment under specific statutory schedules.Fines and Restitution: Monetary penalties or compensation orders are occasionally leveraged alongside custodial sentences to account for damages.
